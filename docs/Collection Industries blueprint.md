## 1. Executive direction

Collection Industries (CI) is a Namibia-based **Business Intelligence and Strategic Solutions Company**. Its commercial entry point is **data analytics, automated reporting, and dashboard development**. Financial intelligence, risk analysis, business advisory, and technology support that core offer.

CI should not try to launch as a bank, credit bureau, investment adviser, lender, or government funding institution. It is an independent solutions company that helps organisations understand their data, make better decisions, and improve operational and financial readiness.

The immediate business objective is to win paid work, prove delivery, and build repeatable services before investing heavily in a large software platform.

### Strategic priorities

1. Sell business intelligence and reporting services to organisations with real operational data problems.
2. Build a focused support offering for entrepreneurs preparing funding applications and strengthening their businesses.
3. Develop institutional relationships, including opportunities connected to the National Youth Development Fund (NYDF), without implying endorsement, appointment, or partnership unless formally agreed.
4. Turn repeatable delivery processes into internal software and reusable client tools.
5. Expand into a broader multi-company CRM and, later, selected ERP capabilities only when revenue and usage justify the complexity.

## 3. Target markets

CI has two connected customer groups. They should be served through different workflows and offers.

### Market A — Institutions and established organisations

Potential customers include public institutions, development programmes, SMEs, financial services firms, retailers, logistics operators, property businesses, and other organisations that need reliable reporting or decision support.

**Typical problems**
- Reports are assembled manually from spreadsheets.
- Management cannot see performance in one place.
- Data is inconsistent, duplicated, or poorly structured.
- Financial and operational performance is difficult to monitor.
- Decisions are made without timely evidence.

**Entry offer:** Data analytics, automated reporting, and dashboard development.

### Market B — Entrepreneurs and funding applicants

This segment includes entrepreneurs, start-ups, and SMEs preparing for funding opportunities, including applicants who may be engaging with the National Youth Development Fund (NYDF).

**Typical problems**
- Weak or incomplete business plans.
- Unclear budgets, cash-flow assumptions, or financial projections.
- Limited understanding of costs, margins, and operational requirements.
- Poor record-keeping and limited performance reporting.
- Difficulty presenting a credible, evidence-based business case.

**Entry offer:** Business-readiness support, business plans, budgeting, standard financial projections, and practical business analysis.

CI may support applicants independently. It must not represent itself as an NYDF agent, evaluator, guarantor, or official service provider unless a formal agreement authorises that role.

The promotional price is campaign-specific, not the permanent price. Record campaign dates, eligibility, and expiry before advertising it. Do not silently include advanced modelling or advisory in the basic package.

### Quotation and revenue rules

1. A draft quotation is not a sent quotation.
2. A sent quotation is not an accepted quotation.
3. An accepted quotation is not an invoice.
4. An invoice is not proof of payment.
5. Revenue and cash receipts must be tracked separately.
6. A sale should only be recorded according to a defined business event, such as accepted work or recognised delivery, not merely because a draft quote exists.
7. Payments must be recorded against invoices, with part-payments and outstanding balances supported.
8. Discounts and campaign prices must be traceable to an approved campaign or authorised override.
9. Institutional engagements require a documented scope, deliverables, assumptions, milestones, and payment terms.

## 6. Sales and client acquisition system

CI's initial sales motion is a combination of direct prospecting, relationship-led outreach, and inbound visibility.

### Sales workflow

1. Identify a target organisation or entrepreneur.
2. Research the organisation and identify a relevant contact.
3. Record the lead source, segment, need, contact details, and next action.
4. Send a tailored introduction or service offer.
5. Schedule a follow-up call or meeting, typically on the next working day where appropriate.
6. Qualify the problem, urgency, budget, decision-maker, and data availability.
7. Prepare a scoped proposal and quotation.
8. Follow up and record the outcome.
9. On acceptance, create the project and delivery plan.
10. Invoice, record receipts, close the engagement, and request feedback or a referral.

### Pipeline stages

- New
- Contacted
- Responded
- Qualified
- Meeting scheduled
- Proposal in preparation
- Quotation sent
- Negotiation
- Won
- Lost
- On hold

The CRM must keep lead status, quotation status, project status, invoice status, and payment status as separate fields or linked records. One status must not be used as a substitute for another.

### Prospecting discipline

- Build a verified prospect list rather than collecting random email addresses.
- Record where contact information came from and when it was checked.
- Use relevant, honest, personalised outreach.
- Respect opt-outs and applicable privacy and electronic-communications requirements.
- Marketing automation must require human approval before a campaign or bulk outreach is sent.
- Do not scrape, store, or use personal data without a lawful purpose and appropriate safeguards.

## 7. Demonstration portfolio

CI should build reusable demonstrations using synthetic, public, or properly authorised data.

### Priority demo concepts

**Logistics intelligence**
- Delivery volume and on-time performance.
- Route or region comparisons.
- Cost per delivery and delay analysis.
- Exceptions and operational bottlenecks.

**Project performance**
- Budget versus actual.
- Planned versus actual progress.
- Milestones, delays, and cost variance.
- Risk and issue tracking.

**Retail business intelligence**
- Sales by product, branch, and period.
- Gross margin and product performance.
- Stock movement and low-stock indicators.
- Customer and basket trends where lawful data is available.

### Reusable upload-to-insight workflow

The long-term reusable workflow should be:

1. Client uploads a supported spreadsheet or data file.
2. System validates file type, size, structure, and required columns.
3. User maps source columns to a defined data model.
4. System profiles the data and flags missing values, duplicates, and anomalies.
5. User confirms the transformation rules.
6. Processing generates standard KPIs and analysis.
7. Dashboard and report are produced.
8. User reviews and approves the output.
9. Results are saved to the correct client workspace with an audit trail.

Do not promise fully automatic interpretation of every arbitrary spreadsheet in the first release. Start with supported templates and clearly defined schemas. Human review remains necessary for financial or high-impact conclusions.

## 9. CRM product blueprint

The current repository is **Collection-Industries-CRM**. The CRM is intended to become CI's internal operating system first, with controlled client-facing access where useful.

### Product principles

- Integrated architecture.
- Internal and external access, with distinct permissions.
- Multi-company support so the platform can serve multiple legal entities or client organisations.
- Department-aware access and workflows.
- Human approval for outbound marketing.
- Auditability for important business and financial actions.
- Build in stages; do not attempt a full ERP before the CRM is useful.

### Core user groups

- Platform owner / system administrator.
- Company owner / administrator.
- Manager.
- Sales and business development.
- Analyst / project delivery.
- Finance / accounts.
- Read-only reviewer.
- External client or applicant, where a client portal is enabled.

Permissions must be role-based and company-scoped. Access to one company's records must not expose another company's records.

### Core CRM modules

| Module | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Companies and workspaces | Separate companies, teams, and client workspaces |
| Users and memberships | Invitations, roles, and access control |
| Departments | Organise teams and internal ownership |
| Contacts | People and organisation contacts |
| Leads | Prospect tracking and pipeline management |
| Service catalogue | Standard CI service definitions |
| Campaigns and prices | Approved promotional offers and pricing history |
| Quotations | Draft, sent, accepted, rejected, expired |
| Invoices | Billing and due dates |
| Payments | Receipts, allocations, part-payments, balances |
| Projects | Delivery scope, owners, milestones, status |
| Documents | Controlled storage and document metadata |
| Financial models | Versioned models, assumptions, and outputs |
| Audit events | Record important changes and user actions |

### Future ERP modules

Only consider these after the CRM and delivery workflows are stable and used consistently:
- Accounting integrations and fuller finance workflows.
- HR and employee administration.
- IT and asset/service management.
- Procurement and inventory where there is a real operating need.
- Advanced business intelligence and AI-assisted analysis.

## 10. Technical direction

The initial CRM foundation uses:
- Next.js and React for the web application.
- TypeScript for application code.
- Tailwind CSS for styling.
- Supabase/PostgreSQL as the planned authentication and database layer.
- GitHub for source control and review.
- Vercel as a potential deployment platform.

This is the intended architecture, not a claim that production services are already connected or deployed.

### Architecture principles

- Keep secrets on the server; never expose a Supabase service-role key in browser code.
- Use company-scoped records and row-level security (RLS).
- Enforce least privilege. A normal member must not automatically have administrator rights over invoices, payments, financial models, or user management.
- Validate inputs on the server as well as in the interface.
- Log important financial, access, and document events.
- Use migrations and reviewed pull requests for schema changes.
- Separate development, test, and production environments.
- Back up data and test restoration.
- Store client documents in private storage with access checks and short-lived access links.
- Do not place real applicant financial documents in an untested demo environment.

### Data and privacy safeguards

- Collect only data needed for a clear business purpose.
- Define retention and deletion rules.
- Restrict access to sensitive financial and identity documents.
- Encrypt data in transit and use platform-supported encryption at rest.
- Keep an audit trail for document access and material changes.
- Establish incident response and backup procedures before onboarding sensitive client records.
- Confirm applicable Namibian privacy, financial, consumer, and sector-specific legal obligations with qualified professionals.

## 11. Delivery operating model

Each engagement should have a repeatable lifecycle:

**Discover → Scope → Quote → Approve → Collect data → Validate → Analyse → Review → Deliver → Invoice → Collect payment → Close**

Every project should have:
- A named client and responsible contact.
- A written scope and exclusions.
- Deliverables and acceptance criteria.
- Data sources and permissions.
- Assumptions and limitations.
- Owner and review responsibility.
- Timeline and milestones.
- Fee, invoice schedule, and payment terms.
- Final files and handover record.

For analytical work, preserve reproducibility: keep source versions, transformation notes, calculation logic, assumptions, and output versions.

- Define initial database schema and multi-company direction.
- Document architecture, commercial rules, and security requirements.
- Keep demo data clearly labelled.

- Invoices, payment allocation, and balances.

### Phase 4 — Project delivery and client workspace
- Project creation from accepted work.
- Milestones, owners, deliverables, and documents.

### Phase 5 — Analytics workflow
- Supported file uploads and data validation.
- Reusable transformation templates.
- KPI calculations and dashboard generation.

- Accounting or other integrations where justified.

## 15. Risks and decisions still requiring validation

| Risk / open decision | Required action |
|---|---|
| No confirmed institutional contract or NYDF partnership | Treat NYDF as a target ecosystem, not an existing partner |
| Broad service catalogue may dilute sales | Lead with BI and dashboards; sell supporting services when relevant |
| Limited team capacity | Prioritise a small number of repeatable offers |
| Arbitrary spreadsheet formats | Start with supported templates and explicit validation |
| Sensitive applicant data | Complete security, access, retention, and legal review before collection |
| Pricing may not cover delivery effort | Track hours, direct costs, rework, and margin per engagement |
| Promotional offer may be misunderstood | Publish eligibility, scope, start date, and expiry |
| CRM scope may become too large | Deliver phased milestones and require evidence before adding ERP modules |
| Role permissions may be too broad | Review RLS and test each role against each sensitive table |
| AI-generated analysis may be wrong | Add validation, explain assumptions, and require human approval |

## 16. Definition of success

CI is succeeding when it has:
- A clear market position understood by prospects.
- A small number of services that customers repeatedly buy.
- Demonstrable analytical work with documented methods.
- A working sales process with disciplined follow-up.
- Reliable delivery and positive client references.
- Cash collected, not merely quotations issued.
- A secure CRM that supports real operations.
- A justified path to expand into software and broader business systems.

**The operating rule:** sell and deliver a narrow, valuable service first. Build software around proven workflows. Do not confuse a large platform roadmap with a functioning business.
